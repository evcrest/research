# X thread — Nigeria-first equity opportunity screen

**Source note:** [Full research publication](../../publications/2026/2026-11-12-nigeria-first-equity-opportunity-screen.md)  
**Author:** Evans Jep | **Publication:** Everest | **Status:** Draft

**1/** Nigeria's equity market has had a powerful 2026 run. But a rising index is not a reason to buy indiscriminately.

I screened a starter universe of NGX-listed companies and selected international names for deeper research.

**2/** NGX Group reported the All-Share Index up 62.23% YTD as of 10 November 2026.

That is context, not a forecast. After a strong advance, valuation, liquidity and downside discipline matter.

**3/** Four names move to deeper diligence: GTCO, Zenith Bank, Dangote Cement and Seplat Energy.

This is a research-priority list, not a buy list. No entry prices or expected returns have been established.

**4/** GTCO: reported 9M PBT of ₦879.9bn, group CAR of 34.2% and Stage 3 loans of 4.0%.

But PBT was down 2.3% y/y. Next: normalize earnings, test asset quality and compare valuation with peers.

**5/** Zenith Bank is on the deeper-work list for earnings resilience and dividend sustainability.

The tests: asset quality, capital, FX/rate sensitivity, normalized ROE, dividend cover and valuation.

**6/** Dangote Cement reported Q3 revenue of ₦1,260.3bn (+16.4% y/y), EBITDA of ₦578.0bn (+19.6%) and PAT of ₦336.3bn (+50.9%).

The question: how much of that growth is already reflected in the share price?

**7/** Seplat reported 9M free cash flow of US$749m and working-interest production averaging 144,999 boepd, up 7% y/y.

Stress-test commodity prices, production, operating risk, capex, net debt and dividends.

**8/** Four remain on the watchlist: Airtel Africa (FX and capex), UBA (country and credit risk), MTN Nigeria (debt and cash flow), and BUA Foods (input costs and valuation).

**9/** International names for further research: VOO/VTI, Microsoft, Apple and NVIDIA.

Broad-market funds and individual technology companies carry different risks. Verify Nigerian account availability, custody, fees and transferability.

**10/** Small-account discipline: keep emergency cash outside equities; avoid leverage, CFDs and options; watch fixed fees and FX spreads; diversify by underlying risk; separate trading from long-term capital.

**11/** The biggest gap is valuation. Current official prices, liquidity, normalized peer multiples, updated filings and verified broker costs still need to be assembled.

This is a research queue — not an allocation plan.
