## Executive summary

This screen establishes a starting research universe for a Nigerian-based investor considering shorter-term opportunities and long-term ownership. Nigerian listed equities are the primary universe. International securities are included only where a plausible Nigerian access route was identified; product availability, custody, costs and account terms must still be checked directly before funding.

NGX Group reported the All-Share Index up **62.23% year to date as of 10 November 2026**. That is market context, not a forecast and not evidence that every listed security is attractively priced. After a strong advance, entry valuation, liquidity and downside discipline matter.

The first-pass priority list for deeper diligence is **GTCO, Zenith Bank, Dangote Cement and Seplat Energy**. **Airtel Africa, UBA, MTN Nigeria and BUA Foods** remain on the watchlist. These are research priorities, not buy ratings. The available evidence is insufficient to rank them by expected return or establish attractive entry prices.

## Evidence and interpretation

**GTCO.** The Q3 2026 release reports nine-month PBT of ₦879.9bn, total assets of ₦18.37tn, group capital adequacy of 34.2% and Stage 3 loans of 4.0%. PBT was down 2.3% y/y. Capital and asset-quality metrics warrant deeper work, but earnings composition, impairment coverage, FX effects and valuation remain critical.

**Dangote Cement.** The Q3/nine-month release reports Q3 revenue of ₦1,260.3bn (+16.4%), EBITDA of ₦578.0bn (+19.6%) and PAT of ₦336.3bn (+50.9%). Growth must be tested against valuation, cash conversion, volumes, energy costs, leverage and the possibility that expectations already discount the improvement.

**Airtel Africa.** H1 results to 30 September 2026 report constant-currency revenue growth of 20.8%, Nigeria revenue growth of 24.4% and 193.9m customers. Assess FX translation, capital intensity, debt and cash conversion.

**Seplat.** The 9M 2026 results report free cash flow of US$749m and average working-interest production of 144,999 boepd, up 7% y/y. Cash generation remains sensitive to commodity prices, production reliability, operating conditions and capex.

## Small-start principles

- Keep emergency reserves and near-term spending money outside equities.
- Avoid leverage, CFDs and options while capital and process are developing.
- Avoid concentration in one company, sector, currency or country.
- Consider liquidity and total transaction costs before small trades.
- Keep short-horizon trading separate from long-term capital.
- Reinvest dividends only after considering costs, taxes and valuation.

Total costs may include deposit/withdrawal charges, FX spreads, commissions, statutory levies, custody and taxes. Fixed minimum charges can make small trades uneconomic.
